Key Finding: Geographic Cluster of Supply Risk

100% of the top 10 riskiest combinations are located in just two warehouses: Pune (`W01`) and Nagpur (`W03`). Across these nodes, average on-hand stock covers only 5.06 to 5.46 days, while regional suppliers require 9.0 to 9.7 days to deliver replenishments. This creates an unbridgeable 4-day stockout exposure window whenever weekly orders accelerate.

Section E External Public-Health Context

Epidemiological patterns, monsoon disease dynamics, and the correlation with pharmaceutical demand.

Monsoon Precipitation & Flooding
Late-Monsoon Inundation (July–August 2025)
Maharashtra experienced severe rainfall bursts and urban waterlogging in late July and August 2025. Contaminated municipal water supplies and urban drainage overflow triggered acute public health concerns across western Maharashtra and the Vidarbha region.
Waterborne Illness Spike
368,000+ Acute Diarrheal Cases Reported
State health department surveillance recorded a three-fold increase in clustered waterborne outbreaks between Q1 and August 2025 (rising from 8 to 27 active clusters). Clinical management guidelines dictate aggressive fluid and electrolyte replacement through Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS).
Vector-Borne Transmission
Dengue & Viral Fever Surge in Pune & Mumbai
Municipal health bodies in Pune (PMC) and Mumbai (BMC) reported sharp surges in dengue, chikungunya, and non-specific viral fevers throughout August and September. Fever management relies strictly on Paracetamol (avoiding NSAIDs to mitigate hemorrhage risks).

Analytical Governance: A Timing Match Is Not Proof of Cause

While the temporal (August–September 2025), geographic (Maharashtra cities), and categorical (Hydration + Antipyretics) alignment between reported epidemiological outbreaks and Mediflow order spikes is exceptionally strong, correlation does not establish definitive causation.

Other unmeasured commercial and supply chain variables could produce an identical pattern:

  • Wholesale distributor trade schemes or pre-monsoon volume discount stocking promotions.
  • Institutional tenders or state procurement orders fulfilled through commercial channels.
  • Retail pharmacy automated restocking algorithms reacting to localized sales spikes and amplifying replenishment batches (the bullwhip effect).

Section F Strategic Recommendations

Four data-traced operational actions to eliminate stockouts and fortify future surge resilience.

1
Safety Stock Policy
Recalibrate Safety Stock in Pune & Nagpur from 5 to 14 Days
Data Trace: In Section B, top risk combinations had only 5.06–5.46 days of cover against 9.0–9.7 days lead time. In Section D, this caused fill rates to collapse to 76.64% in Nagpur and 83.27% in Pune.
Effective July 1st annually, adjust the inventory policy for the 6 high-risk SKUs (`P01`–`P05`, `P14`) in Pune and Nagpur, lifting target on-hand stock from ~150 units to 14 days of forward cover (~400 units) so buffer stock comfortably exceeds supplier replenishment cycles.
Owner: VP Supply Chain Target: Complete by Q2
2
Network Optimization
Establish Mumbai (W02) as a Regional Overflow Buffer Hub
Data Trace: Mumbai has 18,000 monthly capacity but operates at only 2.4% utilization (434 units/mo) and maintained a 95.3% fill rate while neighboring Pune suffered 211 backorders.
Pre-position 1,000 units of Hydration and 500 units of Pain & Fever in Mumbai by late July. Create an expedited 24-hour inter-warehouse transfer corridor from Mumbai to Pune (only 150 km) to absorb sudden regional demand shocks.
Owner: Logistics Director Target: July 15 Go-Live
3
Supplier SLAs
Enforce Expedited 4–6 Day Supplier Lead Times During Surge Months
Data Trace: Supplier lead times averaged 9.0 to 9.7 days for high-risk SKUs in B, creating a severe 1.8x risk ratio. Domestic suppliers in the database demonstrate capability down to 4.0 days.
Contract seasonal supplier SLAs for July 15 – September 30 requiring guaranteed delivery within 4 to 6 business days. Pre-qualify secondary regional manufacturers in Maharashtra to handle surge overflow orders.
Owner: Head of Procurement Target: Contract Sign-Off
4
Demand Sensing
Deploy Automated +30% Order Run-Rate Early-Warning Triggers
Data Trace: In Section A, August Hydration demand accelerated by +81.4% MoM (1,304 units), yet reorder delays caused the peak crisis in September (412 backorders).
Implement demand-sensing alerts in the ERP: if rolling weekly order run-rate in August exceeds baseline by ≥ 30%, automated replenishment triggers will immediately generate production orders across all 4 Maharashtra facilities.
Owner: IT & S&OP Lead Target: ERP Integration
